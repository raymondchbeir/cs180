Overview

Three monochrome exposures become one color photograph.

The digitized glass plates contain three grayscale exposures stacked vertically in B, G, R order. Each exposure records the same scene through a different color filter.

Because the exposures are slightly displaced, directly stacking them produces colored ghosting. I keep the blue channel fixed, estimate the translation of the green and red channels relative to blue, shift them, and then stack the aligned channels into an RGB image.

Part 1

Single-Scale Alignment

01

For the low-resolution JPEGs, I exhaustively search translations in a ±15 pixel window. For every candidate displacement, I shift the entire channel and score only the interior region so the plate borders and wraparound from np.roll do not dominate the match.

L2 distance

Σ (B − Gshifted)²

L2 compares raw pixel intensities. The best alignment is the displacement with the smallest squared difference.

Normalized cross-correlation

Σ(B−B̄)(G−Ḡ) / √(Σ(B−B̄)² Σ(G−Ḡ)²)

NCC removes each channel's mean intensity before comparison. The best alignment is the displacement with the largest correlation.

Part 2

Coarse-to-Fine Image Pyramid

02

The TIFF scans are much larger, so a large exhaustive search at full resolution is expensive. I recursively downsample each channel by a factor of 2 until the image is small enough for a ±15 pixel search. I then return up the pyramid, doubling the estimated displacement at each level and refining it locally within a ±5 pixel window.

1/8 scalecoarse search
1/4 scale2× shift + refine
1/2 scale2× shift + refine
full scale2× shift + refine
Why this is faster

A displacement that may be 100 pixels at full resolution becomes only about 12–13 pixels at 1/8 resolution. The coarse level finds the neighborhood of the correct solution cheaply, and the higher-resolution levels only need small local searches.

Failure analysis

Emir of Bukhara

Why the baseline fails here

The Emir image is my one retained failure case. The three filtered exposures have substantially different intensity distributions, especially across the subject's clothing. Raw-pixel NCC assumes that corresponding image structure produces correlated intensity patterns, and that assumption is weaker here than in the other provided images.

An edge- or gradient-based feature could reduce sensitivity to these cross-channel intensity differences, but I kept the required raw-pixel NCC baseline and documented the failure rather than adding an image-specific correction.
